Research Proposal
Title of the Dissertation 
Development of an Autonomous AI Deployment System for API Management: A Zero-Code, Industry-Agnostic Approach to Enterprise AI Integration
Research Problem
Existing Gaps in Automated AI Deployment Systems:
Despite significant advancements in AI model development and orchestration, current deployment systems exhibit several limitations. Most frameworks rely heavily on manual configuration, scripting, and DevOps integration, which restricts accessibility and scalability. According to Sinha and Lee (Sinha & Lee, 2024), industrial AI applications often suffer from fragmented deployment pipelines, lack of semantic interoperability, and insufficient support for dynamic adaptation across domains.
Moreover, existing platforms rarely support full-cycle automation—from intent recognition to API generation and deployment—without human intervention. This gap is particularly evident in enterprise contexts where rapid prototyping and cross-functional collaboration are essential. The absence of intuitive, language-driven interfaces further limits the usability of AI deployment tools for non-technical users.
Theoretical Background
The theoretical foundation of this dissertation draws upon multiple concepts from computer science, organizational theory, and human–computer interaction. Together, these frameworks provide the basis for the development of a zerocode, industryagnostic AI deployment system for API management.
Software Engineering and Automation Theories
Zero‑code API systems are closely linked to theories of automated software engineering. Approaches such as Model‑Driven Engineering and Automated Code Generation explain how declarative specifications or natural language inputs can be transformed into executable code (Feurer et al., 2015; Wu, Shen & Lin, 2023). These theories underpin the automation of API generation and deployment.
Human–Computer Interaction (HCI) and Cognitive Load Theory
A central element of zero‑code platforms is usability. Theories of Human–Computer Interaction (Norman, 2013) and Cognitive Load Theory (Sweller, 1988) explain why visual interfaces and natural language inputs reduce complexity and increase accessibility for non‑technical users. This democratization of API development enables broader participation and accelerates innovation (Sahay, Singh & Gupta, 2021).
Natural Language Processing and Intent Recognition
The theoretical basis for translating natural language into technical API specifications lies in NLP models such as BERT (Devlin et al., 2019) and GPT (Brown et al., 2020). These models enable intent recognition, i.e., the semantic identification of user intentions, and thus form the foundation for zero‑code API deployment (Krishnan, 2025).
Systems Theory and Asynchronous Data Flow Models
The Data Flow Theory and concepts of asynchronous processing (Tadi, 2024) provide the theoretical basis for resilient and scalable architectures. They explain how APIs can be generated and deployed in real time without centralized control, while minimizing latency and error propagation.
Sociotechnical Systems Theory and Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
Zero‑code API implementations are not only technical innovations but also organizational ones. The sociotechnical systems theory (Trist & Emery, 1973) describes the interaction between technology and organizational structures, explaining how acceptance and efficiency emerge. Complementarily, the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) (Davis, 1989) provides a theoretical framework for studying user adoption, particularly through the dimensions of Perceived Ease of Use and Perceived Usefulness.
Significance of Research
The growing complexity of digital infrastructures and the rapid proliferation of APIs and AI services across industries demand new paradigms for system deployment—particularly those that can be operated by nontechnical users. This study is significant because it addresses both practical enterprise challenges and broader societal impacts.
Contributions to Enterprise AI Integration
By introducing a zerocode framework for API management, this research demonstrates how business units can independently configure and deploy APIs without requiring advanced programming skills. This reduces reliance on IT departments, accelerates innovation cycles, and enhances organizational agility (Zhao, Chen & Wang, 2021; Gartner, 2023).
Democratization through Natural Language Processing
The integration of Natural Language Processing (NLP) creates a semantic bridge between human language and machine‑readable logic. This enables intuitive interaction with complex systems and democratizes access to AI technologies, thereby expanding participation across diverse organizational contexts (Q. Liu, Zhang & Huang, 2022).
Practical Implications Across Industries
Logistics: Faster implementation of APIs for realtime tracking, supply chain optimization, and partner integration (Schmidt & Bauer, 2020).
Financial Services: Secure and compliant API deployment for payment automation, thirdparty integration, and adherence to regulatory frameworks such as PSD2 and MiFID II (Keller, Meier & Novak, 2023).
Healthcare: Seamless integration of AI services such as diagnostic support and patient communication, while maintaining strict compliance with GDPR and HIPAA (Organization, 2022).
Societal and Economic Impact
In the long term, this study contributes to digital inclusion by lowering technological barriers and enabling broader participation in AIdriven processes. Enterprises can operate more efficiently and develop new business models based on flexible API usage and intelligent automation (Bandali, 2024; ShoutOutUK, 2024).
Research Questions and/or Hypotheses
Hypothese – Usability & Accessibility
H1: A zerocode API design framework based on natural language input will significantly increase accessibility for nontechnical users compared to traditional API management tools.
Hypothese – Organizational Impact
H2: The design of a zerocode API system will lower the skill threshold required for enterprise API management, thereby fostering innovation and reducing reliance on specialized IT teams.
Research Methodology

This study adopts a Design Science Research (DSR) methodology, which is particularly suitable for projects focused on the conceptual design of technological frameworks rather than their full technical implementation. DSR emphasizes the creation and evaluation of artifacts—such as models, architectures, and mockups—that address identified research problems and contribute to both theory and practice (Hevner et al., 2004).
Research Design
The research design is qualitative and exploratory, combining design-oriented artifact development with empirical validation through expert feedback and usability evaluation. The central artifact is a conceptual framework and mockup of a zero-code, industry-agnostic API deployment system.

Data Collection
Systematic Literature Review: A structured review of existing scholarship on zero-code platforms, API management, NLP-driven intent recognition, and multi-agent protocols provides the theoretical foundation (Webster & Watson, 2002).

Expert Interviews: Semi-structured interviews with IT architects, compliance officers, and API developers are conducted to validate the conceptual framework and assess its feasibility in enterprise contexts (Myers & Newman, 2007).

Usability Evaluation with Mockups: Mockups of the proposed system are presented to non-technical users (e.g., business analysts). Feedback is collected via questionnaires using Likert scales to measure perceived ease of use and usefulness (Davis, 1989).

Data Analysis
Qualitative Content Analysis: Expert interview transcripts are analyzed to identify recurring themes and validate design principles (Mayring, 2014).

Descriptive Statistics: Survey data from usability evaluations are analyzed to quantify user perceptions of accessibility and organizational impact.

Triangulation: Findings from literature, expert interviews, and user surveys are cross-validated to ensure reliability and robustness.
Validation and Reliability
Since no full implementation is carried out, validation focuses on conceptual soundness and user acceptance. Reliability is ensured through transparent documentation of design decisions, systematic coding of interview data, and replication of survey instruments.

Ethical Considerations
All data collection activities (interviews, surveys) are conducted in compliance with GDPR. Participation is voluntary, and responses are anonymized to protect privacy.

Data Sources and Rights of the Participants
Data Sources
The primary data sources for this study will be:

Systematic Literature Review: Academic publications, conference proceedings, and industry reports on zerocode platforms, API management, NLPdriven intent recognition, and multiagent communication protocols. These sources provide the theoretical foundation and contextual background for the proposed design framework (Webster & Watson, 2002).
Expert Interviews: Semistructured interviews with IT architects, compliance officers, and API developers. These participants are selected for their professional expertise in enterprise API management and AI deployment systems (Myers & Newman, 2007).

Usability Evaluation of Mockups: Feedback from nontechnical users (e.g., business analysts, project managers) who will interact with mockups of the proposed zerocode API design system. Their responses will provide insights into accessibility, usability, and organizational impact (Davis, 1989).
Participants
Participants will be drawn from two main groups:

Technical Experts: Professionals with experience in API design, enterprise architecture, and compliance management. Their role is to validate the conceptual framework and assess its feasibility in real enterprise contexts.
NonTechnical Stakeholders: Business analysts, managers, and endusers without programming expertise. Their role is to evaluate the usability and accessibility of the mockup designs, ensuring that the system meets the needs of nontechnical participants.
Rights of the Participants
Voluntary Participation: All participants will be recruited on a voluntary basis, with the option to withdraw at any time without consequence.

Informed Consent: Participants will be provided with clear information about the study’s purpose, procedures, and expected contributions before giving consent.

Confidentiality and Anonymity: Data collected from interviews and surveys will be anonymized. No personal identifiers will be disclosed in the dissertation or related publications.

Data Protection: All data will be stored securely and handled in compliance with GDPR regulations. Access will be restricted to the researcher and supervisory team.

Respect and NonExploitation: Participants’ expertise and time will be respected, and their input will be used solely for academic purposes.

This approach ensures that the study adheres to ethical standards while generating reliable insights into the design of a zerocode API deployment system.

Legal Issues
Protection of Participants’ Rights
The study will adhere strictly to established ethical and legal standards in research. Participants’ rights will be protected through the following measures:

Voluntary Participation: All participants will be recruited on a voluntary basis. They will be informed that they may withdraw at any time without penalty.

Informed Consent: Prior to participation, individuals will receive a clear explanation of the study’s purpose, procedures, and expected contributions. Written consent will be obtained before any data collection (Myers & Newman, 2007).

Confidentiality and Anonymity: Personal identifiers will not be collected or disclosed. Data from interviews and surveys will be anonymized, ensuring that individual responses cannot be traced back to participants (Creswell & Creswell, 2018).

Data Protection Compliance: All data will be stored securely in passwordprotected files and handled in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Access will be restricted to the researcher and supervisory team (Voigt & Von dem Bussche, 2017).

Respect and NonExploitation: Participants’ expertise and time will be respected, and their input will be used solely for academic purposes.
Securing the Right to Use Data

The right to make use of the collected data will be secured through:

Formal Consent Agreements: Written consent forms will explicitly grant the researcher permission to use anonymized data for academic analysis and publication.

Institutional Oversight: The research will be conducted under the supervision of Signum Magnum College, ensuring compliance with institutional ethical review standards.

Restricted Use: Data will be used exclusively for the dissertation and related academic outputs. No commercial use will be permitted.

Data Retention Policy: Data will be retained only for the duration of the research project and securely deleted thereafter, in line with GDPR requirements.

This framework ensures that participants’ rights are fully protected and that the researcher has the legal and ethical authority to use the data for academic purposes.
